01 / The Problem

One in five patients never shows up.

Across 110,527 appointments at clinics in Vitória, Brazil, 20.2% of patients simply did not appear — costing clinics time, resources, and lost care for other patients.

That's over 22,000 missed appointments in a single dataset.

02 / Age

Youth skips. Age shows up.

Patients in their 20s and 30s miss appointments at the highest rates. The trend reverses sharply after 50, where patients are far more consistent.

Patients aged 20-29 are 40% more likely to miss than those aged 60-69.

03 / Wait Time

The longer you wait, the less you show.

Same-day appointments have a near-zero no-show rate. As wait time grows, the chance of absence increases significantly.

Appointments scheduled 30+ days out see no-show rates spike above 35%.

04 / SMS Reminders

The SMS paradox.

Counter-intuitively, patients who received an SMS reminder showed up less often. This is due to reminders being sent mostly to those with long wait times.

Timing matters more than the reminder itself.

05 / Health Conditions

Chronic illness drives commitment.

Patients with hypertension or diabetes consistently show lower no-show rates. Chronic conditions create a higher sense of urgency.

Health literacy is a powerful predictor of attendance.

Key Takeaways

What the Data Tells Us

Shorten Wait Times

Wait time is the strongest predictor. Shortening gaps can cut no-shows in half.

Target Young Adults

Patients aged 18–35 are the highest-risk group. They need tailored intervention.

Fix the SMS System

Reminders are sent too late. Earlier, smarter communication is required.

Focus on High-Risk Areas

A few neighbourhoods account for most no-shows. Target these areas first.

Leverage Chronic Care

Use the reliability of chronic patients to model new attendance strategies.
